How It Works

1

Choose your PDF

Pick the PDF whose pages you want as pictures, for example a contract or a form you need to post as photos.

2

Set the JPG quality

Use the slider from 10% to 100%. The default of 90% is a good starting point; lower it for smaller images.

3

Every page is converted

Each page becomes one image. To get only some pages, cut them out first with Extract Pages.

4

Save your images

A one-page PDF gives a single .jpg. A longer PDF gives one ZIP file with page_1.jpg, page_2.jpg and so on.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I convert just one page of a PDF to JPG?

The tool always converts the whole file. To keep a single page, first cut it out with Extract Pages, then convert that one-page PDF. It saves as a plain .jpg, with no ZIP.

What resolution are the JPG images?

Each page is drawn at twice its PDF size, roughly 144 DPI. A standard A4 page comes out at about 1,190 by 1,684 pixels. The tool has no separate DPI setting.

What JPG quality setting should I use?

The slider goes from 10% to 100% and starts at 90%. Values around 80 to 90% usually look close to the original on screen. Going lower makes smaller files but can blur small text and sharp edges.

Why did I get a ZIP file instead of images?

A PDF with several pages is saved as one ZIP named after your file, with page_1.jpg, page_2.jpg and so on inside. Only a single-page PDF is saved directly as a .jpg.

Should I choose JPG or PNG for my PDF pages?

JPG files are usually smaller, which suits photos and scans you want to send in a chat or email. For pages with lots of text or fine lines, PDF to PNG keeps every pixel without compression loss.

Will the text in the images be editable or searchable?

No. A JPG is just a picture of the page, so text, links and form fields turn into pixels. Keep the original PDF if you still need to select text or fill in fields.

Can I convert a scanned PDF with this tool?

Yes. A scanned page is already an image inside the PDF, and it is drawn the same way as any other page. If the scan was low resolution to begin with, the JPG cannot add more detail.

Are my PDF pages uploaded to convert them to JPG?

No. Your browser draws each page and encodes the JPG on your own device. The images and the ZIP are built locally, and nothing is sent to a server.
